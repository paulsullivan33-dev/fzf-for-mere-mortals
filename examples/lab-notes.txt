The lab — one real binary, one invented tree, nobody else's files
====================================================================

The lab is fzf as the project ships it, run on the
build machine (an Ubuntu 24.04 sandbox, 2 CPUs,
7.7 GB RAM, bash 5.2):

  * fzf 0.74.5, the project's official release binary
    for Linux amd64, unpacked into the sandbox. The
    package manager was locked by another process for
    the whole lab, so apt never got its turn; the
    binary is the same build the installer of
    chapter 3 would have fetched.
  * A practice tree built for the guide
    (fzf-lab/build-sample-tree.sh rebuilds it exactly):
    27 files in 3 folders — documents/ (14 invented
    texts: two annual reports, two budgets, two
    meeting notes, a letter, two recipes, a shopping
    list, a holiday plan, two invoices, a readme),
    music/ (8 song names), photos/ (5 photo names).
    File contents are two to four lines each; three
    documents mention cinnamon, one of them with a
    capital C — on purpose, as t90 shows.
  * Every measured claim was made through fzf's
    scriptable face (--filter and friends), because
    printed output can be kept and checked. The lab's
    shell history, for chapter 6, is the sandbox's
    own; the history widget itself was verified
    present, not exercised by keystroke.

Exit codes in transcripts are printed as "[exit N]"
lines by the capture harness immediately after the
command that produced them.

What the lab could not do
--------------------------------------------------------------------
  * Drive the interactive window. The sandbox has no
    terminal a person can type into for the record,
    so the window, its keys, the preview pane and
    Ctrl-R / Ctrl-T / Alt-C are taught from the
    project's documentation and the integration
    script — and labelled documented-only in
    criteria.md wherever they appear.
  * Press Esc in a window, so exit 130 is documented,
    not measured.
  * Install through apt (locked), or on Windows or
    macOS at all. Chapter 3 teaches those routes from
    the project's documentation.

The stumble worth keeping
--------------------------------------------------------------------
The first feeder for chapter 7 was written without
grep's -i. The shopping list writes "Cinnamon" with a
capital C, so grep printed two lines instead of
three, and an fzf search for "shopping" then exited 1
on a line it had never been given. The guide teaches
the fix (-i) and keeps the failure as t90, because
"the feeder decides the universe" is easier to
believe with the evidence attached.
